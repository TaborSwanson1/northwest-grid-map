- **2026-11-10 (Day 2, clean data).** Set up the repo. `src/fetch.py` saves every raw file untouched. `src/plants.py` builds `data/clean/plants.csv` (452 plant and type rows, 441 plants, 50,752 MW, matches the generator file) and `plant_owners.csv` (MW split by percent owned; shares checked to add to 100%). Side of the Cascades is by county: 18 OR and 19 WA counties count as west. Result: 76.2% of capacity is east. Wind 98% east, solar 84%, hydro 83%.
  Parents: wrote `data/reference/parents.csv`, one source per row. Wind, solar and battery MW by parent in OR and WA: Avangrid 2,286, Portland General 1,110, NextEra 1,082 (five LLC names: Stateline, Vansycle II, Wheatridge, Wheatridge East, Sundial battery), Brookfield 845 (Shepherds Flat), Puget Sound Energy 774, PacifiCorp 450, EDP Renewables 407. Left under their own names because sources disagree: Windy Flats (Cannon Power?), Washington Wind (Clearway or Turlock Irrigation District?), White Creek (investor group with utility buyers). Arlington Wind Power Project is matched to EDPR's Rattlesnake Road by county and size only; confirm.
  Lines: `src/lines.py` checks every HIFLD line against OpenStreetMap by sampling a point every 500 m and finding the nearest OSM line within 150 m. 85% of HIFLD lines in OR and WA match an OSM route, and 74% of those have the same voltage class. Most disagreements are at the low end (under 100 vs 100 to 161 kV). 500 kV: 144 of 158 matched pieces agree; the other 14 are short pieces at the Grand Coulee and Chief Joseph switchyards where 500 and 230 kV lines run side by side, so HIFLD is probably right. Fixes: four Tacoma Power lines near Mossyrock and Mayfield that HIFLD calls 240 kV DC are 230 kV AC in OSM, so they're recoded AC. The DC Intertie's 1,000 kV is pole to pole; labeled ±500 kV. Added BPA's Big Eddy to Knight 500 kV line from OSM (44 km), which HIFLD doesn't have. Skipped BPA's Lyons test line and an experimental HVDC line.
  Substations: 2,628 in OSM, 1,319 with a voltage, 68 at 500 kV including Celilo, Big Eddy, John Day, McNary, Slatt, Grand Coulee, Raver, Echo Lake and Monroe. Seven 500 kV ones have no name.
  Next: Day 3, the interactive map.
- **2026-11-10 (Day 3, interactive map).** `src/build_web.py` turns the clean data into small GeoJSON files in `docs/data` (2.3 MB total): plants, lines (simplified to about 50 m), substations at 230 kV and up, counties with 2025 population, the west/east county line, and Natural Earth land, rivers, lakes, borders and cities. Rivers had to include Natural Earth's "Lake Centerline" pieces, or the dammed stretches of the Columbia and Snake disappear. `docs/index.html` is a MapLibre map: soft hillshade from AWS Terrain Tiles, plant icons by type colored by parent owner (Avangrid, Portland General, NextEra, Brookfield, Puget Sound Energy, everyone else gray), AC lines darker and wider as voltage rises, the DC Intertie dashed purple with a "To Los Angeles: 846 miles, 3,220 MW" label where it leaves Oregon (-119.96, 42.00). Filters for plant type and voltage class; 500 kV and DC on by default. Click popups for plants, lines, substations and counties. Layers are added on style load, not full load, so plants don't wait on terrain tiles. Checked in the browser at desktop and phone width; no console errors. Load speed couldn't be judged because the preview pane was hidden, which slows the page down.
